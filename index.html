Sustainable Transformations: an interactive narrative tool

Student consultant for a Nordic sustainability consultancy (client anonymised), capstone of the MA Creative Sustainability, 2024

An interactive narrative tool for Finnish mid-cap companies that exemplified how leadership mindset change could manifest.

  • 30+Finnish mid-cap companies benchmarked
  • 7transformation pillars

My role

In the team, my role was varied, but mainly focused on the conceptualisation and development of the deliverable.

An original framework

No existing framework covered everything organisational sustainability transformation involves, so the team built one. We synthesised PESTLE criteria, B-Corp standards and sustainable business model research (Geissdoerfer et al., 2018) into a visioning structure with 7 pillars, benchmarked across 30+ Finnish mid-cap companies.

An interactive narrative tool

We developed the content and visual concept for a tool with 3 scenes, each grounded in the 7-pillar framework. The format was chosen for its potential to cause the cognitive and emotional shifts needed to start systemic change.

Validated against science

The design is aligned with 10 sustainability visioning criteria (Wiek & Iwaniec, 2014), ensuring validity and direct integration into the client's digital services and stakeholder presentations.

Climate performance metrics

Led the GHG and climate performance metrics: researched and proposed decarbonisation calculations, target-progress measures and emission intensity ratios, benchmarked with the GHG Protocol, SBTi and ESRS E1. I also worked on team-wide methodology beyond my assigned topic.

Exploring the desirable futures of land use

Master's thesis, MA Creative Sustainability, Aalto University, study area in Manizales, Colombia

Converted land-use tensions into actionable future scenarios, enabling 23 stakeholders across public, private and academic sectors to share views and chart pathways.

  • 23stakeholders engaged
  • 7future scenarios co-created
  • 2co-creation workshops
  • 5/5thesis grade

Independent end-to-end research

I designed and led the research with municipal, civic and private actors, then facilitated 2 co-creation workshops to synthesise insights into 7 speculative future scenarios addressing housing, mobility, ecology, governance and urban-rural dynamics.

Built with the people involved

All 7 scenarios reflect the community's own ideas. My role as a researcher was to create the conditions for ideating and developing concepts despite the tensions between land-use interests, then synthesise them into coherent, actionable proposals.

A reusable futures toolkit

I designed one toolkit with two workshop formats (A-type and B-type), so that local actors can run independent back-casting sessions beyond the academic engagement.

Siena: atmospheric water collection

Bachelor's graduation project, Universidad de los Andes, 2022

A low-cost atmospheric water collection prototype that translates natural mechanisms of páramo plants into design principles.

  • 3plant mechanisms turned into prototypes
  • 5/5grade, Outstanding Project at the Volarán exhibition
  • SDG 6, 15Clean Water and Life on Land

Nature as design intelligence

Applied biomimicry and biodesign methods to identify water-capturing adaptations in páramo plant species. I translated three selected mechanisms into physical prototypes: hydrogel structures (moss absorption), fog-catching surface geometries (frailejón leaves) and capillary networks (plant vascular systems).

Research and recognition

I conducted a literature review across ecology, plant physiology and natural materials, complemented by conversations with 5 experts, including botanists, soil scientists and ecosystem specialists. The material exploration and prototype development were carried out independently. The project was awarded Outstanding Project at the Volarán exhibition at Universidad de los Andes.
